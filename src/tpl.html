Three packages

Each package is a matched set — logo, typefaces, palette and homepage drawn together. The mark sitting in each site header below belongs there, and would look wrong in either of the others. Pick one package rather than parts.

Package I

Ivory

Mark: The Notch · After Tom Ford

A geometric mark drawn from the lapel notch, set against warm ivory with black used like ink and antique gold on the details. The highest perceived price of the three.

The mark — The Notch

The idea

A plain frame with one corner of the top edge cut at the angle of a lapel notch — the gap where collar meets lapel on every tailored jacket ever made. Off-centre, because a real notch is off-centre.

Why it is his

Nobody else can use it. A crown belongs to everyone; a lapel notch belongs to tailoring. A man who wears suits reads it before he can explain it.

The honest risk

It asks for confidence. At a glance it is a rectangle with a nick in it, and it only fully pays off once it sits on a label beside an actual lapel.

Package II

Savile

Mark: The Roman · After Huntsman

Roman numerals and an old-style serif on warm bone, with oxblood for accents. The package that looks as though the house has been trading for forty years.

The mark — The Roman

The idea

Twenty-one written the way a watch dial or a cornerstone writes it. XXI is near-symmetrical, so it sits square between two rules with no optical correction.

What it fixes

Written as 21, the number reads like a street address or a jersey. Written as XXI it reads like a house that has been there a while.

The trade-off

The least surprising mark on the page. It will never be mistaken for anyone else's, but it will not stop anyone in the street either.

Package III

Chrome

Mark: The Label · After Zegna

No icon at all — the name alone, on pure white with true black and no accent colour anywhere. The most modern of the three and the cheapest to keep looking sharp.

The mark — The Label

The idea

No mark at all. The name in a true Didone, one hairline rule doing the only decorating. A garment label enlarged and promoted to become the whole identity.

Why it works

The most expensive-looking and the cheapest to produce. Céline, Saint Laurent and Balenciaga all threw away their marks and kept only the name.

The trade-off

No small-format fallback — no icon for a profile picture, an app tile or a lapel pin. The initials 21 stand in wherever the full name will not fit.

Part Four

What I'd choose

Package I, Ivory. The notch gives him a mark that is genuinely his own and cannot turn up on a competitor's window; the ivory ground keeps the whole site readable on a phone in daylight, which is where most clients will first see it. The other two are real options, not filler — here is when each wins.

The recommendation

I · Ivory

Ownable mark, highest perceived price, strongest on a phone. This is the package I would build.

The safe build

II · Savile

Choose this if the client is nervous about looking new, or is selling mainly to older professional buyers who want a house that already looks established.

The modern build

III · Chrome

Choose this if the buyer skews young — first suit, first real job, groomsmen. Cheapest to run and photograph, but it gives up a proper icon.
