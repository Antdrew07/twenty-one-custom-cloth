Part Two

The cut

Four silhouettes the house will offer, drawn as tailor's flats. These same drawings become the style selector on the website, so a client chooses his cut before he ever walks in.

Two-button · Notch

The Single-Breasted

The working suit. Notch lapel, two buttons, flapped pockets. Reads correctly from a courthouse to a client dinner.

Six-on-two · Peak

The Double-Breasted

Authority. Peak lapels, six buttons with two fastening, always worn closed. The hardest jacket to buy off a rack — the best argument for made-to-measure.

Five-button · Waistcoat

The Three-Piece

Jacket, trouser and waistcoat. One extra fitting, a whole new silhouette. Weddings live here.

One-button · Satin peak

The Dinner Jacket

Black tie. Velvet or barathea, lapels faced in satin, one button, no vents. Shawl collar on request. Sold once and worn for twenty years.

The three lapels — the detail that decides the jacket

Notch

The default. A step cut where the collar meets the lapel. Business, daily, unremarkable in the best way.

Peak

Points sweep upward toward the shoulder. Formal, broadening, and standard on every double-breasted jacket.

Satin facing

The evening tell. Lapels faced in satin against matte cloth — the one detail that separates a dinner jacket from a dark suit.

Part Three

Three packages

Each package is a matched set — logo, typefaces, palette and homepage drawn together. The mark sitting in each site header below belongs there, and would look wrong in either of the others. Pick one package rather than parts.

Package I

Ivory

Mark: The Greca Seal · After Versace

The Greca Seal — XXI inside a laurel wreath, ringed by the house name and a thirty-unit Greek key — set against warm ivory with antique gold. The richest object of the three, in the world built for evening.

The mark — The Greca Seal

The idea

Three concentric rings placed by radius: laurel, name, Greek key. The key is one hook repeated thirty times so it closes on itself without a seam. Est. 2026.

Why it reads expensive

A seal is complete in itself — it stamps on a box, foils on a label and engraves on a button with nothing added. The Greek key is the one border every luxury house understands.

The honest risk

Its richness lives in fine detail, so it needs size. Below about 40 px the key becomes texture; the header uses it at 46 px, which is the floor.

Package II

Savile

Mark: The Arms · After Burberry & Huntsman

The Arms — coronet, shield bearing XXI over the motto Ad Mensuram, laurels, and Custom Cloth on the ribbon — on warm bone with oxblood. Heraldry in the world where heraldry belongs.

The mark — The Arms

The idea

A full heraldic achievement on a strict grid: five-point coronet, eighteen laurel leaves placed by angle, a ribbon with folded tails. XXI above, Custom Cloth below — the whole name without a word repeated.

Why it reads expensive

It is what his current shield was reaching for, done properly. Ad Mensuram — “to measure” — is real Latin, real to the trade, and his alone. Burberry’s Prorsum is the precedent.

The honest risk

The most elements to reproduce. Embroidered small, the leaves and the motto will need simplifying — the woven tile shows where it tightens.

Package III

Chrome

Mark: The Cartouche · After Vuitton

The Cartouche — the numerals 21 inside a quatrefoil frame with a fleuron in each notch — on pure white and true black. The most modern of the three marks in the most modern of the three worlds.

The mark — The Cartouche

The idea

Four lobes of one radius, the notch points solved by geometry so the outline closes cleanly. A double rule and four fleurons do all the decorating around a Didone 21.

Why it reads expensive

Restraint and ornament. It carries the frame of a Vuitton flower with the type of a fashion house — and it is the only one of the three that turns into a repeat pattern for lining silk.

The honest risk

The quatrefoil is a known Vuitton shape. This drawing is his; the association is theirs. It reads as lineage to most eyes and as borrowing to a few.

Part Four

Three houses, three crests

The marks are settled: the Greca Seal, the Arms and the Cartouche, each drawn on a grid for this house alone and each set in the world it belongs to. What remains is the choice of world. Ivory sets the highest price and photographs best in the evening; Savile builds the repeat business the plan is written around; Chrome reads youngest and runs cheapest.

The recommendation

I · Ivory × The Greca Seal

The richest mark in the richest world. Complete on a box, a bag, a label and a button, and the strongest showing on a phone.

The heritage build

II · Savile × The Arms

Coronet, shield, laurels and a Latin motto in the world English tailors have used for two hundred years. Choose it for older professional buyers.

The modern build

III · Chrome × The Cartouche

A monogram in a quatrefoil on pure white. Choose it for the first-suit buyer, and for the lining pattern it turns into.
